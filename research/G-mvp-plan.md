# G · MVP implementation plan

## Build one mission from evidence to outcome

The vertical slice opens on a mixed-use city block and includes City Dispatch with one complete task 2.2 mission, two to three authored stakeholders, an original technical debrief, optional Exam Lens, a held-out independent check, and the first delayed probe. Give the city block distinct schools, restaurants, offices, shops and homes, with brief location and resident previews. Give City Dispatch a playable cutaway interior. Let players discover the first job from the map and resume it directly. Include the system editor, event playback, deterministic comparison and saved impact. Use task 2.2 in the slice and reserve the multi-agent propagation variant for expansion. [CCAR-F guide task 2.2](https://github.com/Amey-Thakur/CLAUDE-CERTIFICATIONS/blob/main/architect-foundations/exam-guide.pdf), [B · Mission](B-game-design.md), [C · Visual direction](C-visual-direction.md)

**Essential** is a city block with different kinds of places and one complete decision, feedback and measurement loop. **Optional** is generative NPC dialogue, a second playable location, and more elaborate animation. **Later investment** is additional neighborhoods and deeper daily routines, standalone currency or economy, real-time multiplayer, automated content publishing, live tool execution, a calibrated readiness headline and additional full certification packs. The first slice should demonstrate charm and technical judgment in the same room. [Chernikova et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320933544), [Anthropic, Building Effective Agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)

## Evidence plan

**Usability and game loop.** Run two rounds of approximately 5–8 representative learners with mixed Claude experience. Observe whether they identify a symptom, inspect the appropriate case, place a rule, understand the consequence and choose to try another case. An initial design gate is at least 8 of 10 learners completing the key flow unaided and explaining one cause from a replay, with no critical accessibility blockers. Treat that as a team threshold, not a population estimate. Track first decision time, intervention edits, unassisted completion, voluntary replay and next-mission choice. Compare exploring the city to find a first job with resuming an active assignment directly and analyze time to the first meaningful action. [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [Chernikova et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320933544)

**Comparative pilot.** Recruit working professionals with relevant interests, including exam-eligible partner candidates. Pre-register one primary outcome before looking at results. Suggested outcome is score on independently authored, delayed transfer cases 7–14 days after a time-matched intervention. Randomize within prior-experience blocks and cohorts where practical. Compare the mission with an active condition using the same concepts, elapsed study budget, worked examples and practice feedback in conventional scenario material. Give both groups a brief parallel pretest, a separate immediate post-test and a delayed form. Fix the assessment content and grade rationale with a blinded, anchored rubric on a sample. Include near-transfer and less similar cases and report them separately. Record open Lens and hint exposure as secondary variables. Keep identical reminders and approved accommodations. [Butler, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20804289/), [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/), [Testing Standards](https://www.apa.org/science/programs/testing/standards)

Begin with a feasibility sample to estimate attrition, time and score variance. A study with 60–100 total randomized learners is directional unless the observed uncertainty supports the intended inference. For scale planning, detecting a standardized difference of 0.5 with 80% power and a two-sided 5% test takes roughly 64 analyzable people per arm under simple independent assumptions. Inflate for attrition, clustering and a smaller material difference. Predeclare a minimal meaningful difference with the SME, for example 10 percentage points on a suitably reliable transfer form, and make any go decision explicit about interval width. Report effect, interval, baseline balance, exclusions, differential attrition, missing-outcome sensitivity and subgroup limitations. If the effect is inconclusive, revise or continue measuring before making efficacy claims. [Testing Standards](https://www.apa.org/science/programs/testing/standards)

**Decision on expansion.** Expand the authoring effort when the main flow works unaided, delayed transfer is promising versus active prep, a second mechanic fits without stretching the engine, and an eligible partner will fund a pilot. At a final release gate, require credible evidence against the preregistered meaningful difference or a justified engagement gain with no material learning loss. If the scene adds friction and the schematic performs better, keep the institution framing and simplify the route to the task. If players enjoy the mission but miss technical transfer, revise the debrief and evidence prompts before adding scenery. If optional dialogue adds little, keep authored dialogue. [Wouters et al., 2013](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-cognitive-and-motivational-effects-of-seri/), [Sundararajan and Adesope, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)

**Commercial discovery.** Interview partner program leads and learners about time-to-certification, procurement, existing free training, eligibility and the usefulness of cohort reporting. Offer a concrete paid pilot with defined seats, study duration and feedback. Keep individual demand on a separate free demo track. A useful gate is one paid pilot and two independent qualified prospects agreeing to evaluate, followed by a contribution check after support, content time and inference. This is an initial threshold. A signed pilot and actual engagement carry more weight than survey intent. [Anthropic partner certification launch](https://claude.com/resources/articles/four-role-based-claude-certifications), [CertGrove pricing](https://certgrove.com/pricing), [AWS Cloud Quest](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/training-and-certification/introducing-the-new-aws-cloud-quest-ai-powered-practice-hands-on-building-and-a-path-to-official-aws-badges/)

## Phased roadmap

Roles are effort assumptions. One person may cover several, but assessment review and critical technical verification require an independent reader. Discovery and buyer recruitment start early, before the more expensive phases.

| Phase | Deliverable and dependency | Staffing assumption and risk | Acceptance or stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Research-informed prototype** | One mixed-use city block, one depot cutaway, a small route interaction, and early visual and mechanic tests. Depends on guide verification and core fixtures. | Product/game designer, frontend/game engineer, part-time art and Claude SME. Risk of polish before diagnosis works. | Target players locate the problem and make a meaningful rule change. Confirm camera, layout, and performance plan on named devices. |
| **2 · Playable vertical slice** | Complete end-to-end task 2.2, financial model, optional Lens, evidence and delayed check. Depends on deterministic evaluator and reviewed content. | Two engineering skill sets, one art generalist, learning specialist/SME and QA, some part-time. Risk of platform scope or brittle feedback. | Golden model and accessibility checks pass, resume survives errors, first comparative pilot is feasible. Start payer and learning validation here. |
| **3 · Foundations pathway** | Add jobs in settings such as a school, restaurant, research archive and corporate office with several mechanics and original assessment families. Depends on slice validation and a second mechanic. | Content lead, Claude SME, independent reviewer, art and engineering. Risk of content throughput and drift. | Every guide bullet mapped to a reviewed teaching/evidence artifact. Report gaps transparently until the coverage and independent item gates pass. |
| **4 · Product validation** | Run confirmatory learning comparison and paid partner cohorts. Measure engagement, unit economics, accessibility and readiness evidence. | Research/measurement owner and partner lead, with QA and operations. Risk of selection bias and long follow-up. | Meet preregistered transfer or engagement guardrails, track contribution and demonstrate repeated partner value. External exam prediction stays gated by independent validation. |
| **5 · Certification expansion** | Choose a second official pathway after checking eligibility and current guide. Developer Foundations is a reuse candidate if a partner needs it. | SME for new exam and existing simulation and assessment staff. Risk of hidden code execution or grading needs. | One existing institution and at least two mechanics reused with correct new content. Compare authoring time and independent performance before a full pack. |
| **6 · Platform expansion** | Constrained Studio, cohort controls and broader institutions after repeated pack releases. Add optional real labs or cross-institution models only for measured needs. | Content operations, additional art/engineering and governance. Risk of multiplying support and maintenance. | Non-engineer authors can make a reviewed variant and safely publish/roll back. Operational and content costs fit sustained demand. |

The phase order preserves the requested pathway and brings a small comparative pilot forward to reduce the cost of a wrong assumption. Technical thresholds, prices, staffing and sample sizes remain planning hypotheses. See [H](H-risks.md) for owners and reversal signals.
