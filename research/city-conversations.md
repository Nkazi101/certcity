# Conversations in CertCity

Reviewed November 12, 2026. FarmVille 3 is the mobile reference. Zynga’s current store listings identify the app on Android and iOS. Its help screenshots show Marie introducing a feature in a short speech card while the farm and the relevant object remain in view. The Lotus Pond guide shows a named specialist introducing herself and the work she handles. The public help material establishes the pattern. This review used published footage and screenshots. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/farmville-3-farm-animals/id843506607) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en-US&id=com.zynga.farmville3) · [Merchant walkthrough](https://zyngasupport.helpshift.com/hc/en/91-farmville-3/faq/15399-how-do-i-use-the-merchant/) · [Lotus Pond walkthrough](https://zyngasupport.helpshift.com/hc/en/91-farmville-3/faq/22289-new-crafting-station-lotus-pond/)

CertCity uses the same staging principle with original characters, portraits, and dialogue. The city stays visible while a person introduces a concrete problem. Players ask for evidence, choose replies, and see how those choices affect the existing replay. The stakeholder explains the result and the case file remains available at each decision. The technical debrief and short skill check stay at the location after a successful run. The skill check uses a different setting and records its own result. Optional domain practice opens from the check review. City Dispatch introduces T-17 through three staff members and hands the player into the existing interactive workflow. School, restaurant and corporate assignments use their original deterministic scoring and browser saves.

Landmark contacts without a scored assignment offer short visits. The first authored visits feature Central Library and Olive & Rye. Other locations use the place profile as a two-step introduction. Eight original Blender portraits support the four playable assignments and those two preview locations. The portraits are rendered stills. The city remains a live 3D scene, with authored dialogue and selectable replies.

The design hypothesis is that tying a decision to the person who needs it helps players find the next action and remember the evidence. Observe whether new players find a speaker from the map, discover a constraint without a hint, and explain how their reply changed the result. Include keyboard and touch users. The browser interaction tests establish that the flow works. First-time participant observation remains the validation step.
