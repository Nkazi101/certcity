# FarmVille visual comparison

I used Zynga’s published FarmVille 2 gameplay imagery for the visual comparison because it shows a rendered, interactive world at a similar camera angle. The production sources include an environment artist’s asset breakdown, a principal artist’s account of the look development, and Zynga’s GDC postmortem. They explain how asset treatment and the everyday activity of the setting fit together. [Zynga gameplay](https://www.zynga.com/games/farmville-2/) · [Frank Vanderwel’s asset breakdown](https://frankvanderwel.artstation.com/projects/y4ZqJ) · [Tony Trujillo’s look development](https://www.tonytrujilloart.com/farmville2-1) · [Zynga’s GDC postmortem](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1018015/FarmVille-2-Postmortem-What-Grew)

## Why the first 3D build fell short

| Observation | Change in this build |
|---|---|
| The first 3D build had six selectable locations. It reused one house silhouette for 45 background homes. Many street views repeated that shape, even when wall and roof colors changed. | The Downtown build expanded to four districts and 17 selectable places. Residential rows now use gable, cottage, townhouse and flat terrace forms. Downtown adds a stepped Art Deco tower, a curved corner office, brick warehouses, a classical library, glazed offices, and low storefronts. |
| Asphalt took about 39% of an ideal repeating street block. At the default zoom, roads and empty setbacks occupied foreground space where FarmVille 2’s published view shows distinct productive areas, recognizable props, and visible activity. | Maplewood’s repeating streets now use about 33% of the same 24-unit grid. The foreground in Downtown and Market Row includes a sculpture pool, benches, outdoor tables, market stalls, plant trays and raised growing beds. Downtown uses its own street widths. |
| The initial scene relied on flat color and broad shared materials. Vanderwel describes painting asset-level color and shading, including a baked ambient-occlusion contribution that preserves color in shadow. A global light cannot supply that variation on its own. | Distinct materials now include mottled terra cotta, brick, roofing, transparent greenhouse panes, and individually varied window bays. Leaf geometry carries subtle vertex shading. The art is still primarily modeled and shaded in code, with shared textures. |
| Early street life was dominated by people traversing loops. The first depot assignment could change the numbers while the support desk looked the same. | Downtown and Market Row add people working at stalls and plots, gathering at a café, and visiting a plaza. The same simulated shift result drives the support desk’s visible ticket stack as well as its workload readout. |

The street comparison uses (1-(1-w/24)^2) for orthogonal roads of width (w), with 5.2 and 4.3 world units for the before and after. It excludes sidewalks, uses the layout dimensions, and does not estimate whole-frame pixel coverage. The composition observations compare a published FarmVille 2 screenshot with the first 3D build at the closest readable zoom. They do not establish a quality score. ## Downtown St. Louis in CertCity

- **Park Pacific.** The playable landmark at 1226 Olive uses the former Missouri Pacific building’s stepped, U-shaped tower, two-story base, west entrance and relief, vertical ribs, pale terra cotta and blond-brick rear. The renovation photos informed the visible annex. The asset compresses scale to keep the whole silhouette readable at street exploration zoom. [National Register nomination, section 7](https://mostateparks.com/sites/mostateparks/files/Missouri%20Pacific%20Bldg.pdf) · [St. Louis Public Library historic photo](https://cdm17210.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/local_scenes/id/341/) · [Renovation photos](https://www.paric.com/project/park-pacific-2/)
- **District layout.** The grid borrows Olive, Locust, Pine, Chestnut, Washington, 13th and Tucker. Locust Offices borrows the Shell Building’s rounded limestone corner. The library, warehouses, plaza and café are original interpretations with fictional occupants and tasks. Downtown’s street lengths, parcel sizes and neighboring blocks are compressed to suit the game. ## What remains to reach the visual standard

The largest remaining difference is asset finish. Park Pacific has a bespoke silhouette and façade, but its lighting is still mainly shared scene lighting. The city’s small storefronts, rooftop equipment, trees, and residents use fewer silhouettes and authored material details than the benchmark shows. A fair next comparison would hold the camera and block composition fixed and put one artist-finished exterior, one street-prop group and one character with a distinct rig beside their current versions. That isolates the value of painted textures, better silhouettes and animation from the effect of adding more blocks. Browser checks verified selection, camera movement and layout. Visual quality still needs an independent side-by-side review, and this pass did not measure device-wide rendering performance.
