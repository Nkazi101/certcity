# CertCity student pathway audit

November 14, 2026

This report records the baseline before the workbench redesign. See the [November 20 re-audit](#november-20-re-audit) for the quality of the intervening work, the [assignment experience audit](#assignment-experience-audit) for the interaction-by-interaction findings, and the [mission portfolios](mission-portfolios.md) for the current experience.

## Verdict

CertCity is credible as guided certification practice. I would use it to rehearse decisions and find concepts to study. I would wait for independent evidence before using a completed pathway to decide whether to book an exam. Architect Foundations is the strongest learning experience. Associate Foundations is the best fit for a learner with everyday Claude experience. Developer Foundations needs implementation practice. Architect Professional has the largest gap between what the learner selects and what the role is expected to design and defend.

The navigation and assessment shell are consistent. The work standard varies sharply. Dispatch, Printworks, the receiving counter, and the archive research table let me change meaningful state and inspect consequences. Most other workbench stations accept the same positional answer through three controls, even when the case facts support mixing those controls. The dashboard counts successful replays from both kinds of assignments toward the same “Objectives applied in the city” measure. ## How I tested

The judgments below model what those learners would infer from feedback, remember after repetition, and have to do independently. They are qualitative estimates.

I used separate guest browser contexts and completed every saved replay, debrief, city exam check, and follow-up across the 34 assignments. I checked that the follow-up was scheduled after each check, advanced the browser clock eight days, and submitted the follow-ups through the normal UI. I also opened each optional Exam Lens and verified that it returned feedback. I ran one timed mixed practice session per track, reloaded after saving a response, then exercised each Academy follow-up. Automated traversal used authored keys to verify grading and workflow. I separately made purposeful wrong or incomplete choices and tested unassisted navigation, misleading controls, exposure, and persistence. Scores from traversal reflect the test inputs. Actual learners, real seven-day retention, signed-in cross-device behavior, and performance on the proctored exam remain unmeasured. I visually inspected the rendered Maplewood scene. The full sweep used the DOM fallback with WebGL disabled. I also reviewed the four current English July guides linked by Partner Academy, their public samples, the item banks, and the scoring and persistence code. The repository’s type, certification-content, and city-route checks passed. ## What I could do after each track

### Associate Foundations

**Readiness — good introductory preparation for an active Claude user.** As a staff member I could explain why a family gets the approved May 24 date, why another gets a pending response, and why a late exception goes to the enrollment lead. Sending confirmed replies for everything produced a visible blocked result on two messages. The vendor bulletin and library jobs reinforced source verification, audience adaptation, shared instructions, and review authority. The school pilot's separation of drafting speed from net staff time is a good professional decision. These cases fit the intended role.

I still had no graded example of a prompt I wrote and improved from an actual output, a Project I configured and verified across fresh chats, or an Artifact I edited and checked. The library activity kit asks for an editable staff deliverable and a structured import, then grades a configuration instead of either deliverable. I could follow the prompt strategy on a new vignette more confidently than choose among chat, Research, Projects, Artifacts, and Memory from a realistic workflow with competing needs. Output evaluation is the largest domain at 21%. It deserves more conflicting-source and subtle-output cases where I have to cite what changed and adapt the answer without losing verified facts. The one delayed question shared by several evaluation assignments cannot provide that depth. ## Findings that change the student experience

### 1. The workbench teaches a repeatable answer pattern

The shared instrument appears in 27 missions and 112 stations. Its three apparent controls must have equal indexes before **Apply to case** is enabled. It records that index and uses it to select the current station's prewritten outcome. The generic station key is always the middle option. The other controls and their labels repeat on the following station even when the objective and case change. In the Associate reference desk, I chose a shared Project, put the changing glossary in knowledge, and scoped the connector to approved program data. These are the choices supported by the assignment, but together they were indexed second, first, second. **Apply to case** stayed disabled. Selecting second, second, second enabled it, even though the second glossary option uses last month's email as policy. The replay could then be saved. This can make a careful student abandon correct reasoning and rewards learners who discover the position rule. It also compromises the assignment completion evidence behind the objective count. [Instrument data and apply rule](../components/academy/role-mission-mechanic.tsx#L56) · [Station answer construction](../lib/claude/missions.ts#L32) · [Reference desk case](../lib/claude/role-missions.ts#L33)

The same UI says draft settings save as I work. I set the workspace control, closed the reference desk, and reopened via **Resume work**. The selection was gone. Applied station picks persist. Unapplied field settings live only in the open instrument. [Local instrument state](../components/academy/role-mission-mechanic.tsx#L155) · [Workbench draft storage](../components/academy/mission-workbench.tsx)

### 2. Assessment evidence depends on which door I use first

Across the tracks, 118 of the 125 Academy practice items also serve as city exam checks. In the default mixed sets I reproduced 43 overlaps across 48 slots. City scoring has some exposure protection, but it counts every ID assigned to a practice draft. I opened the Associate mixed set, saw only the first question, exited, and then submitted the Market Hall city check. The city flagged four of that mission's five items as previously seen. Three of the four had never appeared on screen. In the opposite direction, the Academy selection and first-answer counter consult Academy attempts only. A student who completed the city first can receive first-answer credit in the Academy for familiar items. Several city-to-check pairs also reuse the structure and numbers of the teaching case. The Associate notice pilot and its check use the same 12-to-5 drafting minutes, 2-to-6 review minutes, and two corrections out of 30. This is a sound rehearsal exercise with limited evidence of transfer. [Exposure logic](../lib/claude/pathway.ts#L211) · [Academy first answers](../lib/claude/practice.ts#L58) · [School pilot](../lib/claude/role-missions.ts#L44) · [Associate practice items](../lib/claude/associate.ts)

The optional Lens also cuts across that boundary. In 22 of the 33 standard modules the Lens item is used as a check in another module. Five item IDs each recur in two modules’ Lens exercises. Learners can follow the suggested optional practice and still consume a future question. [Lens item selection](../lib/claude/assignment-lens.ts#L36)

The shared four-step progress indicator measures actions. I submitted a 4/5 check and the check milestone became complete. The journal's first-seen performance measure separately excludes misses. That distinction is useful, but a module can reach 4/4 without demonstrating every mapped skill. Current city checks generally provide feedback on the submitted form and no fresh form for recovery. The two advanced Architect Foundations gates allow explanation review to unlock further practice, which is appropriate for progress. It provides a different kind of evidence from answering a new case. [Milestone logic](../lib/certification-progress.ts#L34) · [Challenge prerequisites and evidence](../lib/claude/pathway.ts#L264)

### 3. The seven-day schedule works. The questions stop being held out

The standard follow-ups across 33 missions use 27 distinct Academy items for 68 question slots. Every one of these 27 items is also in its track's Academy follow-up. Dispatch uses its own four-question arts-center scenario. I submitted the same three Associate follow-up questions after both the vendor bulletin and the school notice pilot. The second result still called them held out. Changing the route or the order of missions can therefore turn delayed evidence into a recall test of a rationale the player has just read. The date works as a study reminder when the student returns to that module. There is no portfolio-wide list of due reviews in the city journal. ### 4. The city establishes stakes. Interactivity needs to carry those stakes

The strongest moments connect a person, an inspectable artifact, a genuine control, and a result. Dispatch's T-17 preview and full-week replay do this well. Printworks shows who inherits a rule. The archive retains evidence and exposes missing coverage. The three legacy starters offer small, legible choices with visible case outcomes. Five advanced workload replays make cost, time, or capacity constraints concrete. Case evidence, local consequences, and short role-specific debriefs are worth preserving.

Engagement is likely strongest in the first meaningful replay and declines when the player sees the same dialogue rhythm, same three controls, and same solution across several jobs. Conversations give context but generally offer one scripted question at each beat. Many workbench boards are illustrative panels that already name the expected flow. The city impact summary is predominantly a record of individual completed outcomes. It gives closure, while most later jobs begin with their own authored fixtures. I would describe the experience as varied in setting and uneven in learner agency. That is a walkthrough judgment. Measures of attention, enjoyment, pacing and return rate need learners. [Mission interaction flow](../components/academy/mission-workbench.tsx#L218) · [Replay and city outcomes](../components/certcity.tsx#L302)

The app's 3D fallback preserves access to Assignments and work areas. Most of the browser sweep used that fallback, so this audit supports behavior of the work surfaces rather than a 3D performance or accessibility claim. I confirmed a timed Associate mixed set can be completed at a 390 px viewport and the response and timer survive a reload. Assistive-technology compatibility and touch performance across the city remain unverified.

### 5. The exam contract is clear, with a near-term content deadline

The catalog calls this independent practice, links the official materials, and explains partner eligibility. The exam counts, July guide scope and 120-minute response window matched the official materials. The 12-item sessions are practice drills with optional approximate pacing. CertCity has no full-length independent form, and the Academy practice banks contain fewer unique questions than each official exam's item count. Different question lengths and formats, content breadth and durability remain unmeasured. Answer positions in the Academy are varied and rationales explain the alternatives. Some keys also stand out through phrasing or length. As a small cue check, choosing the longest option on each Associate single-response practice item, breaking ties by display order, gets 19 of 29 correct. Live exam difficulty requires separate evidence. The official passing score is scaled and CertCity scores have no validated conversion to pass probability. ## Priorities

1. **Make each station score the work shown on screen.** Use independently evaluated controls or a single honestly labeled decision. For each job, test a valid mixed-position configuration, a near miss, and persistence after leaving halfway through. Reuse the object/feedback loop from Dispatch and the rebuilt work areas. Fix this before counting the affected stations as applied skill.
2. **Make fresh evidence independent of practice.** Give rehearsal, city checks, follow-ups and mocks their own items. Record exposure when a question appears and apply that record across the city and Academy. Use a fresh alternative after a miss and label repeat practice as such. Show due work and separate replay completion from independently demonstrated skills.
3. **Add one substantial task at each role's actual level.** Associate rewrites and verifies an output in a small Project. Developer completes a fixture-backed API, tool or stream integration and proves it with tests. Architect Foundations diagnoses and repairs an agent across several failure cases. Architect Professional designs a retrieval and rollout plan, then defends or revises it when evidence changes. Existing city scenarios provide the cases and data for these tasks.
4. **Build an exam-date-specific readiness check.** Reconcile the three new blueprints when published. Add unseen, timed forms with exam-appropriate grouping and weighting. Use plausible alternatives that satisfy different parts of the case, and report evidence and weak domains. Run the repository's planned comparative learner pilot with independent delayed items before making any outcome claim. [Existing pilot design](G-mvp-plan.md#L26)

## Assignment experience audit

November 20, 2026. I walked all 34 current city assignments in four clean guest browser sessions, opened the case file where available, used the work controls, and inspected the replay, debrief and check. I reviewed the implementation alongside the walkthrough and compared rendered interiors from the WebGL verification. I used the authored answers for the completion and check tests, except for the separate option-length test described below. The assessments of attention, confusion and confidence are predictions about learners. This audit measured product behavior. It has no data from human participants.

### What a learner is likely to do

The first visit has a clear purpose. A person explains who is waiting and what went wrong. A learner who likes exploring can ask both stakeholders and open the case file. A learner on a deadline can go straight to Work area. The direct route is useful for returning learners. On many first visits it also supplies the deciding facts and sometimes the recommended sequence on the workbench itself. If exploring recovers little evidence the learner needs to use, I expect many learners to take the direct route after the first few missions.

The most engaging moment is when something fails for a reason the learner can discover, change and test. That happens when a Dispatch timeout takes the wrong route, Printworks receives half an event, the reference desk cites an obsolete policy, or the kitchen encounters a schema-valid total it should hold for review. A person, the input evidence, a control and an observable consequence line up. Those are the experiences I would expect a learner to remember. In many other assignments the person and setting establish similar stakes, then the work consists of selecting a written recommendation and reading the expected effect. This supports concept review. It gives the learner little experience finding evidence, constructing the solution or deciding what to test.

| Current interaction | Missions | What success establishes |
|---|---:|---|
| Dedicated work surface with a replay (Dispatch and six labs) | 7 | A learner can configure the modeled workflow and meet the requirements on supplied fixtures. |
| Short, case-specific decision sequence | 3 | A learner can route requests or select a policy against explicit facts and thresholds. |
| Mixed workbench with a custom action at one station | 3 | A learner can execute a specific trace or configuration step alongside recognition-based decisions. |
| Standard choices with a calculated workload | 5 | A selected configuration meets simulated limits for the given staffing, cost, quality or deadline assumptions. |
| Standard choices with authored effects | 16 | A learner can identify the supported recommendation in each vignette. The effect is described in a trace. |

**The current grading rewards a superficial strategy.** Choosing the longest visible response at every station passed **19 of 21** assignments whose controls consist entirely of standard choices. The browser reproduced those 19 successful replays and rejected the other two. Across all 99 standard-choice stations, including the standard stations in the three mixed workbenches, choosing the longest available response or responses selected the authored key at **97** stations. I broke length ties in screen order. The misses were the role for Claude in the community application queue and how to preserve order facts in the vendor rush. The test excludes the dedicated labs, three short case sequences and custom controls. It establishes a cue in the current content and gives no estimate of how many people would exploit it. **Role differentiation depends on what the learner can manipulate.** An Associate can work from policies, messages and documents, a Developer from events and contracts, a Foundations architect from routes and state changes, and a Professional architect from evidence, constraints and handoff decisions. The distinct labs deliver parts of that. The standard workbench often gives all four roles the same response sequence. A bulletin, a tool transcript and an evaluation gate can look different while each accepts the same kind of click. The three versions of live versus batch processing illustrate the opportunity. A Developer should reconcile result IDs and retries. A Foundations architect should route the queues and preserve verified state. The advanced variant should ask the learner to adjust capacity or routing as the operating conditions change. All three currently hand the learner a written strategy. **The interior should carry the case evidence.** In the tested library cutaways, a learner can reach the case file, staff and the workstation. Archive search and the Associate reference desk share much of that layout, while their work screens support different interactions. The task-specific equipment adds identity. The larger opportunity is to make it useful. The event console can expose the affected chunk, the market approval board can let the learner compare the signed update with the draft, and the operations room can show the live manual queue moving when the learner intervenes. Keep the keyboard and touch route to those same actions. The scene then helps the learner see and resolve the problem. [Interior and interaction points](../lib/scene/mission-interior.ts) · [Building dialogue and case file](../components/academy/building-assignment.tsx)

**A clear finish state can overstate what happened.** The journal increments “Objectives applied in the city” for every objective mapped to a saved replay. An objective selected from three written recommendations contributes the same way as a repaired system tested against a trace. The separate first-seen and delayed results are worth keeping. Among the 132 immediate check slots, 118 use item IDs that also exist in their track’s Academy short bank. Prior-exposure tracking helps interpret a result, but a learner who starts with Academy drills can consume an assignment’s fresh check. I would use completion to show what was visited and saved, then give separate credit for a successful work sample and a fresh transfer case. **Debrief quality varies with the work available to explain.** Dispatch can compare concrete tool results. Printworks can show actual fixture output. The standard debrief often reprints each selected action and its consequence before giving the technical note and source. That reinforces vocabulary for a novice, but after five to eight similar stations I expect more experienced learners to skim it. A debrief earns attention when it addresses an actual failure the learner just saw, names the technical concept, and explains what would change under different facts. A check then needs a fresh case where the same principle leads to a new decision. [Assignment debriefs](../components/certification-dialog.tsx)

### Judgment on the product promise

Inventory coverage is broad and the shared lesson structure is sound. The portfolio currently meets a consistent standard for introducing cases, recording completion, and providing technical and exam follow-up. It falls short of a consistent standard for doing role-specific work. The clearest benchmark is an assignment where a learner has to find a consequential piece of evidence, change the object or workflow it governs, see the result on a new input, and explain the outcome in the debrief. A better version of CertCity would preserve the low-friction guided starter, then require that standard in later work. For a redesign, the highest-priority tests of that standard are the activity kit, permit intake integration, migration review and operations handoff. They expose the largest gap between the task named on screen and the action the learner takes.
